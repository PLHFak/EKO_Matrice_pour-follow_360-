Abstract

The dedicated SDI output that EKO requires — PGM video or on-air source, international sound, and the three LSM LTCs as audio on channels 14, 15 and 16 — corresponds to a native function of the audio stage of hybrid routers and ST 2110 gateways: the major hybrid families (Evertz, Sirius 800, NVISION 8500, Platinum, UTAH-400, Ultrix) and the gateways whose function is documented compose 16 mono channels per SDI output, destination by destination, and therefore without touching the PGM that leaves the truck, mono granularity remaining not confirmed for MediorNet and FOR-A Evertz EMX3-FR, manual p. 115; GV NV8500, manual p. 69; Lawo .edge, The Broadcast Bridge. It is not, however, available as a matter of course on every output: except on NEXX, Ultrix and MicroN, where embedding is advertised on every port, it depends on embedder output cards and most often on a licence, and in many trucks only some of the outputs are so equipped (finding of the research notes, not sourced truck by truck) GV Sirius 800; GV TN0027-08. No function named "PGM Follow" was found in the public documentation reached for the control systems: following the programme bus is built with rules (GPIO logic and crosspoints in Lawo VSM, "Virtual Tally Router" in TSL TallyMan) or in the vision mixer (K-Frame Bus Linking, Ross Auto Follow, Sony Internal Bus Link according to a third-party training report), with a delay that no manufacturer publishes Lawo VSM, GPIO; TSL TallyMan, manual 02; GV K-Frame, manual v11.0. On a re-embedded router output, transparency to all ancillary data (HANC and VANC) is stated in black and white only for the GV Vega, without ATC being named there; FOR-A documents that its output card passes all ANC with Quiet Switch disabled and passes only a restricted list once that mode is enabled; on the gateway side, only Lawo names ATC: this is the first point to test truck by truck GV Vega, datasheet; FOR-A MFR-3100EX, specification sheet. On price: no public price was found for a router analogue audio input module; the suspicion of a premium over a digital input is therefore neither confirmed nor refuted. What the documents do show is that Sirius 800, NVISION 8500, Ross Ultrix, MicroN and Lawo .edge have no documented analogue input, and that an external embedder with four analogue inputs costs from $275 (Blackmagic, Markertek, seen 2026-10-11) to $1,118 (AJA 3G-AMA, Markertek, seen 2026-10-11) Markertek, Blackmagic CONVMCAUDS2; Markertek, AJA 3G-AMA. The decisive unknown is not the router but the source: the only EVS manual statement found on this point (XT3, Hardware Technical Reference 12.05) says that the Timecode connectors output the LTC "that corresponds to PGM1"; that there is only one output per server is an inference, nothing is confirmed for the VIA generation, and nothing was found on the behaviour of this output in slow motion, in jog or in still frame EVS XT3, Hardware Technical Reference 12.05.

1. Summary and verdict: feasible on most documented architectures, guaranteed nowhere without a test

Implementation (question 1). Three architectures lead to the same signal. In a baseband hybrid router, the input cards de-embed to a TDM bus, the audio crosspoint routes at mono level, and the embedder output card inserts 16 channels per output from any AES, analogue or MADI input Evertz EMX3-FR, manual p. 115. In an ST 2110 truck, the SDI output is rebuilt in the gateway from independent receivers (video -20, audio -30/-31, ANC -40), and embedded audio is excluded by the standard from the -40 stream: "video follows, audio fixed" is the default behaviour there (inference from the research notes, which assumes that the -40 receiver follows the -20 receiver) SMPTE ST 2110-40:2023; VSF IP Showcase NAB 2022, Mailhot. With a transparent router (Blackmagic Videohub, AJA KUMO), an external embedder placed between the dedicated output and EKO is mandatory Blackmagic Videohub, specifications; Markertek, AJA KUMO 3232-12G. Following the programme bus does not exist as a named function in any public documentation reached; it is built from the documented building blocks, and it is the integrator, not the product, that gives it a name (inference from the research notes based on Lawo VSM, GPIO and TSL TallyMan, manual 20).

Market (question 2). The four long-established manufacturers of large routers (Evertz EQX/NEXX, Grass Valley Sirius 800 and NVISION 8500, Imagine Platinum IP3/MX, Utah UTAH-400 Series 2) all offer a mono TDM audio crosspoint alongside the video crosspoint, with 16 embedded channels handled per SDI Evertz EQX-IO; GV Sirius 800, datasheet; Imagine Platinum IP3; Utah-400 Series 2, brochure 2025. In the mid-range, Ross Ultrix is the clearest case of a "router that composes the signal itself", FOR-A MFR-3100EX is, of the five compact families studied together (Ultrix, MediorNet, FOR-A MFR, Videohub, KUMO), the only one to accept analogue natively, Imagine Platinum MX and FOR-A MFR-5000/8000 also having analogue input cards, and Riedel MediorNet places a 16-channel embedder on every port Ross, Ultrix press release 2015; FOR-A MFR-3100EX; Riedel MicroN, datasheet.

Scope and limits (question 3). The function is within reach of a truck equipped with a hybrid router or ST 2110 gateways, and plausible but not confirmed on a MediorNet network, subject to two practical reservations: a free embedder destination, and an input for the three LTCs. MADI is the most general entry point (Ultrix, MicroN and the audio layer of the NVISION 8500 have no documented AES3 input; .edge accepts only 2110-30 and -31 streams); among the families studied, a true in-frame analogue input is documented only at Evertz (EMR cards, in a separate frame), Utah (ADC-400A/12S), Imagine (PT-ADCT-IB) and FOR-A (MFR-8AAI) Evertz EMR-AA; Utah, catalogue 2024-05; Imagine Platinum IP3; FOR-A MFR-3100EX, specification sheet. The price of these modules is published nowhere; external conversion, by contrast, is priced (section 4).

Providers' fleets (question 4). In the NEP, Gravity Media, United States, Canada and United Kingdom inventory of publicised trucks (which also contains a few units from Australia, Belgium, the Netherlands, Germany, France and Qatar), about 60% of the identifiable rows are IP and 25% baseband; in continental Europe, private providers remain mostly on baseband, MediorNet or hybrid, under Lawo VSM in 19 of the 25 rows where the control system is named (counts internal to the research notes, biased towards the trucks that the press and the manufacturers chose to publicise; row-by-row sources in section 7). EMG is an exception with EVS Cerebrum, NEP with its proprietary TFC platform SVG Europe, October 2020; SVG, 8 February 2026.

Verdict. The realistic common denominator is "an SDI output with embedded audio, configured by the truck's control system"; in continental Europe, a native ST 2110 input on EKO would be a convenience for a minority of trucks, not a prerequisite (inference from the research notes, formulated for the continental sample). Five points determine commissioning and are settled by no document: whether ATC survives on the re-embedded output, the delay and behaviour of the follow during transitions, what the LTC output of an EVS server actually delivers away from nominal speed, which remains the most important point, the offset between audio and video on the EKO destination, and the availability of an analogue input for the three LTCs.

2. The function to be built: one destination, three independent essences

The signal expected by EKO is a single SDI output of the router. Its video is the PGM, or, in "PGM Follow" mode, the source that the vision mixer has put on air, taken directly from the router. Its embedded audio carries the international sound on the first channels and, on the last ones (for example 14, 15 and 16), the LTC of each of the three EVS LSM servers, carried as an ordinary PCM audio channel. The composition is done by the router on this destination only: since embedding is performed per output card and independently per destination, the same PGM routed to the transmission output is not affected, provided the LTC is not embedded upstream, for example on the vision mixer's PGM output (inference from the research notes based on GV NV8500, manual p. 69 and Evertz EMX3-FR, manual p. 115).

The point of taking the picture "at the router" rather than at the vision mixer output lies in the ancillary data. In an HD-SDI stream, timecode exists in two forms, ATC_LTC and ATC_VITC, both as ancillary packets on dedicated lines Cobalt Digital, About Timecodes v1.0. The research notes place ATC_LTC in HANC and ATC_VITC in VANC; as embedded audio also travels in HANC, every embedder rewrites the HANC to one degree or another, and the risk is therefore concentrated on ATC_LTC (inference from the research notes). A vision mixer auxiliary output, for its part, is a processed output: ancillary pass-through there is documented in detail only at Ross Ross Carbonite Ultra, Ancillary Data.

The specification thus breaks down into three essences that the router must handle separately: the video and its ancillary data, which follow the source; the international sound, fixed; the three LTCs, fixed. Capacity is not a constraint (inference from the research notes): the gateways whose function is documented (Lawo .edge, GV IQUCP25, Nevion Virtuoso, Sony with NXLL-AM50) embed 16 channels per SDI output, the granularity not being specified for Evertz 570IPG, Imagine SNP and Riedel GV IQUCP25, datasheet; Nevion Virtuoso SDI-IP-H25.

3. How to build it: three architectures, then programme follow

3.1 Baseband hybrid router: a mono route to an embedder output

The mechanism is identical at all manufacturers. At Evertz, the de-embedder card extracts the 16 channels of each of its video inputs to the TDM, the ADMX crosspoint routes "down to the mono channel level", and the embedder card inserts 16 channels per output coming "from any AES, analog, or MADI input module" Evertz EMX3-FR, manual p. 115; Evertz EMR-ADMX. On the NVISION 8500 Hybrid, the router logic chooses, for each of the 16 channels of each output, between the corresponding channel of the video present on that output ("pass-through" flag), generated silence ("null") or an audio source coming from the TDM router GV NV8500, manual p. 69. On the Sirius 800, the AHP 5919 (input) and 5949 (output) modules provide de-embedding, Channel Swap, 16-channel mix and embedding, and any incoming audio (embedded, AES, MADI) can be routed through the audio crosspoint and then embedded GV Sirius 800, manual Iss6 Rev6; SAM Sirius 840, manual p. 143. Imagine Platinum IP3 multiplexes up to 16 channels into each output through its Mux output modules and advertises "full mono breakaway routing"; Utah feeds each embedder output with 16 channels from its TDM audio sub-router Imagine Platinum IP3; Utah-400 Series 2, brochure 2025.

The EKO composite is therefore a textbook case for these embedders: on NV8500, pass-through flag on the channels kept and TDM source on channels 14 to 16; elsewhere, a mono route from the audio crosspoint to the 16 embedder inputs of an output (inference from the research notes). The practical constraint is availability, not capacity: the embedder outputs are a subset of the outputs. At Imagine, the module names indicate mux outputs in blocks of 8 (inference drawn from the "PX-HSR8CMX8C" nomenclature); on NV8500, Dynamic Hybrid Path-finding explicitly targets an architecture of "mostly standard cards with a few hybrid cards"; on Sirius, audio and video processing is licensed per module Imagine Platinum IP3; GV TN0027-08; SAM Sirius 840, manual p. 143. In a busy truck, these outputs are often all allocated (inference from the research notes, not documented).

The configuration is a matter of truck engineering and not of day-to-day operation. The manufacturers present the shuffle as feasible from the normal panels — "Any NVISION control panel can manage channel shuffling and break-away", track-routing dial-up and softpanels at Snell — but creating a dedicated destination with a custom 16-channel map is database work (MAGNUM, Workbench, MRC and NV9000-SE, Magellan, Ucon), typically stored as a salvo or as a virtual destination (inference from the research notes based on Miranda, NVISION 8500 press release, SVG, 22 August 2013 and Evertz EMR, User's Guide rev 1.8). On Ross Ultrix, the only operating procedure found comes from an integrator: "Audio-SDI" panel, selection of the output, then of the channels, "all 16 channels route independently", with capture as a salvo CT Group KB, Ultrix.

3.2 ST 2110 truck: the SDI output is rebuilt in the gateway

In an ST 2110 core, the SDI output is no longer that of a router frame, even though some IP trucks keep an SDI router alongside the IP core (NEP's SS8, F&F's GTX-21, table 7.6): it is recomposed by the gateway from a 2110-20 receiver for the video, several 2110-30/-31 receivers feeding a mono shuffler per output, and a 2110-40 receiver for the ancillary data, each routed independently by the control system. The standard specifies that embedded audio packets "should not be transmitted using this method" in the -40 stream SMPTE ST 2110-40:2023. The model recommended by John Mailhot (Imagine) combines multichannel streams, channel-level routing inside the device, IS-05 for the stream receivers and IS-08 for audio mapping, with a "helper" audio router for the devices that need one; his example is precisely an SDI gateway receiving 16 channels as two 8-channel 2110-30 streams VSF IP Showcase NAB 2022, Mailhot; AMWA IS-08.

The gateways whose function is documented offer this composition, the granularity not being specified for Evertz 570IPG, Imagine SNP and Riedel: on Lawo .edge, "each SDI output on a blade has an Audio Shuffler that can select any mono channel from its 4 IP receivers and embed it into one SDI output"; Grass Valley's IQUCP25 embeds up to 4 audio streams per channel with a "16x16 Audio Shuffle" block; the Nevion Virtuoso accepts up to 16 IP audio streams with per-channel routing, shuffling, delay and gain The Broadcast Bridge, Lawo .edge; GV IQUCP25, datasheet; Nevion Virtuoso SDI-IP-H25. The per-product detail is given in the third table of section 5.

The "PGM Follow" output maps naturally onto such a gateway output: the -20 video receiver and the -40 ANC receiver are switched together to the sender of the camera or server that is on air, and the audio shuffler remains statically patched to the international sound and the three LTCs. This is a standard stream-level breakaway, not an exotic function (inference from the research notes). The point to verify per manufacturer is that the -40 receiver does follow the video receiver, because the source's ATC lives in its -40 stream and not in its video stream; a gateway that switches only the -20, or that treats the -40 as "metadata associated with SMPTE ST 2110-20 video stream" in Grass Valley's wording, may behave differently (inference from the research notes based on GV IQUCP25, datasheet). The text of ST 2110-40 itself names neither timecode nor ST 12-2; timecode "Embedded in the 2110 Ancillary stream" is attested in practice by Pixotope for Matrox cards SMPTE ST 2110-40:2023; Pixotope 25.2, Configure timecode.

Two practical consequences. First, the three LTCs must exist as channels of a 2110-30 stream before reaching the gateway (section 4). Second, two documented examples suggest that IP trucks keep plentiful SDI edges (inference from the research notes): Game Creek's Bravo and Columbia expose a 432 × 528 SDI gateway, the RTBF twins 32 baseband outputs SVG, 25 September 2019; SVG Europe, RTBF.

3.3 Transparent router: an external embedder in front of EKO

The Videohub 12G units are simple SDI crosspoints with reclocking: no embedding, de-embedding or shuffling function appears in the manufacturer's specifications Blackmagic Videohub, specifications. The AJA KUMO units are described by AJA, as quoted by resellers, as routers that "Passes all SDI embedded ancillary data including audio" Markertek, AJA KUMO 3232-12G. The scheme is then: dedicated router output, external embedder that inserts the three LTCs, then EKO. The transmission PGM is not concerned, since the embedder is only in EKO's input path (inference from the research notes).

Only two embedders in the survey are documented as letting the user choose where the analogue audio lands while keeping the rest: the AJA 3G-AMA, whose embedding is "user selectable, on a channel pair basis", and the Lynx Yellobrik PDM 1383, with group selection by rotary switches Markertek, AJA 3G-AMA; Markertek, Lynx PDM 1383. For the Blackmagic Mini Converter Audio to SDI, neither the channels written nor the preservation of incoming embedded audio is specified on the pages read: not confirmed, to be validated on the bench before any recommendation. This model is moreover limited to 3G-SDI; a 12G PGM would require the Teranex Mini Audio to SDI 12G, which has only two analogue inputs, or a model from another brand: the AJA 12G-AMA ("12G-SDI Mini Converter with 4-Channel Audio Embed/Disembed", four analogue channels according to the notes' reading, price not recorded) or the Lynx PDM 1484 D (12G, AES and analogue on D-sub, £1,340.00 excl. VAT at Canford UK, seen 2026-10-11) Markertek, Blackmagic Teranex Mini Audio to SDI 12G; Markertek, AJA 12G-AMA-R-ST; Canford UK. With a transparent router, the source's ATC and audio reach the dedicated output unaltered, and the whole risk of loss shifts to the external embedder (inference from the research notes).

3.4 PGM Follow through the control system: rules, not a named function

In the public documentation reached, no control system markets a one-click "PGM follow" function; the building blocks, however, are documented. In Lawo VSM, a crosspoint can act as a trigger in the GPO logic, a GPO can set a crosspoint, the "Crosspoint as secondary GPI command" window defines a Condition and an Action, and the "Increment Source" and "Increment Target" options generate the series when copying; the documented example chains crosspoint A, GPO, crosspoint B Lawo VSM, GPIO. In TSL TallyMan, "vision mixers are treated exactly as though they are routers", and the "Virtual Tally Router" routes to its single destination the first source in tally, falling back to Source 1 if none is; the example "A 2 x 1 Emergency C/O Router" applies this principle, according to the notes' reading, to a physical router TSL TallyMan, manual 05; TSL TallyMan, manual 02; TSL TallyMan, manual 20.

The generic recipe available in any system that reads the state of the vision mixer's buses is therefore "N rules": for each of the N sources likely to appear on the PGM bus, IF (PGM bus crosspoint = source n) THEN route (router source n to destination "EKO PGM Follow") on the video and ANC level only. This is an inference from the research notes, literally expressible in VSM with the documented condition, GPO, action chain. Keeping the audio fixed while the video follows is a matter of restricting this rule by level or layer: Pseudo Device columns and "Breakaway detection" in VSM, logical levels with shuffle rules in GV Orbit; at TSL, the level mask filters tally channels and not audio and video levels, so that restriction to video is not documented there Lawo VSM, Pseudo Devices; GV Orbit Control, User Guide v1.6.0; TSL TallyMan, manual 02. One limit documented in VSM: Pseudo Device rules do not support paths that pass through tie-lines Lawo VSM, Pseudo Devices.

The rule must be driven by bus state (which input index is on the PGM bus), not by derived tally, which can light several sources during a mix or with keys and then requires a priority rule; and the system must translate "PGM bus = input 7" into "router source CAM 7" through the association tables already used for tally and UMDs (inferences from the research notes). The protocols that expose this state are detailed in section 6.

3.5 Follow inside the vision mixer: tight on timing, uncertain on ancillary data

Internal bus follow is a documented function of the main OB truck vision mixers. The K-Frame v11.0 manual contains the sections "Bus Linking Menu", "Linking Busses one-to-one", "Creating Parallel Bus Links", "Bus Linking Source Substitution Tables" and "Router Control of Aux Buses"; only the titles could be read, not the procedures GV K-Frame, manual v11.0. At Sony, according to a training report from PTS (Taiwan) read in translation and not cross-checked against a Sony manual, the link functions inherited from the MVS series are Internal Bus Link, ME Link and Key Transition Link, with the example of output 1 of M/E 1 linked to Aux 1 PTS, staff paper 2024_003. On Ross Acuity, "Auto Follow" lets an entire Aux bus follow another bus, with an explicit limit: "Auto follow actions only echo commands, and do not echo the state" Ross Acuity, Auto Follow.

An internal link gives the tightest timing and requires no control system (inference from the research notes), but an Aux output is a processed output. Ancillary pass-through is documented only at Ross: on Carbonite Ultra, "The switcher can be configured to strip or pass this data" (Ancillary Mode: Strip or Pass), any format conversion removes the ancillary data, and the frame synchronisers remove embedded audio Ross Carbonite Ultra, Ancillary Data. For K-Frame, Sony XVS/MVS, Kahuna, DYVI and FOR-A, preservation of the source's HANC/VANC on an Aux output is not confirmed. In Pass mode, the source's ATC and embedded audio would pass through together: a downstream embedder would still be needed to overwrite the channels with the international sound and the LTCs (inference from the research notes).

Ross offers a structurally different option: on the vision mixers integrated into Ultrix (HyperMax on an SDPE blade, Ultrix Acuity), "Aux bus routing is done virtually within the router… The video does not pass through the switcher" Ross HyperMax, Aux Bus Router Sources. An Aux that follows the PGM therefore delivers the unprocessed router source there, ancillary data included (inference from the research notes).

4. The LTC chain: the EVS source matters more than the converter

4.1 What EVS servers output: an LTC "that corresponds to PGM1"

The only manual statement found on the analogue LTC output is in the XT3 Hardware Technical Reference 12.05: the Timecode connectors allow the server to receive the reference LTC and to "send the LTC timecode that corresponds to PGM1" (quotation returned in two fragments by the reading tool; neither pinout, nor level, nor connector type visible) EVS XT3, Hardware Technical Reference 12.05. The LTC reader and generator are carried by the MTPC board, according to an XT Series manual from 2005 EVS XT Series, Technical Reference. The output is therefore not per playback channel: three LSM servers give exactly three LTC signals, each describing the PGM1 of its server and that alone; a server with two operators, or with PGM2/PGM3 on air, has no analogue LTC for those outputs (inference from the research notes). By way of comparison, the Grass Valley K2 Summit provides an LTC output "1 per video output" GV K2 Summit, LTC specification.

On the SDI side, the XT3 11.00 configuration manuals document timecode management of "VITC or ANC timecodes on a channel-by-channel basis", with in HD the values LTC, HANC LTC or HANC VITC for the User TC, and an LTC table that automatically receives the LTC EVS XT3 11.00, Configuration Manual p. 135; EVS XT3 11.00, Configuration Manual p. 134. ATC on each SDI output is therefore a class of capability documented on software of the XT2/XT3 era; which table (LTC or User) is inserted on a playback channel could not be read (inference from the research notes, OUT values not confirmed).

Four points remain not confirmed and bear directly on the design:

  • the existence, the connector, the level and the tie to PGM1 alone of an analogue LTC output on XT-VIA, XS-VIA and XT-GO: the Multicam 20.1 manuals exist on the EVS download area but could not be opened EVS download area, Multicam 20.1.39;
  • the behaviour of the LTC output in slow motion, jog, reverse and still frame, whereas clips are played back "at any speed between -400 and + 400%" Wikipedia, Multicam (LSM);
  • the exact values of the "Timecode Insertion Settings" for the OUT channels;
  • any EVS setting that would output the LTC as audio on an embedded channel: nothing of the kind appears in any title or text read.

Looping the LTC output back into an analogue audio input of the server is physically possible only on the frames that have one: the XT3 has "Analog audio: 4 multi-pin DA-15 connectors (2 in and 2 out)", absent from the 4U frame of the XT3 6U, and the XT4K documentation describes no analogue audio I/O EVS XT3, Technical Reference 16.6; EVS XT4K, Technical Reference 15.2. Even where it is possible, an audio input feeds the record trains; no source shows a direct input-to-output path that would place the LTC live on an embedded channel of a playback output (inference from the research notes).

4.2 Limits of the LTC signal: unreadable at standstill, fragile at slow speed

LTC is a biphase-mark signal of 80 bits per frame, i.e. 2,000 bit/s at 25 fps (fundamental from 1.0 to 2.0 kHz) and 2,400 bit/s at 30 fps (1.2 to 2.4 kHz) Philip Rees, Timecode; Wikipedia, Linear timecode. Usual levels range from 0.5 to 2 V peak to peak: Evertz 5010 output "Adjustable, 0.5Vp-p - 2Vp-p", K2 output "2.0 V peak-to-peak differential", Evertz 7800TM2-3G "1V p-p nominal" Markertek, Evertz 5010; GV K2 Summit, LTC specification; Evertz 7800TM2-3G, manual. The rise time is the subject of contradictory sources: 25 µs ± 5 µs according to Philip Rees, 40 µs ± 10 µs according to the libltc documentation and the Evertz 5010 data sheet; the text of SMPTE ST 12-1 was not consulted Philip Rees, Timecode; libltc.

Readers typically track from 1/30 to 70–100 times nominal speed: "between 1/30 and 80 times the nominal rate" for the K2, "1/30th to 70x play speed" for the Evertz 5010, 1/30 to 100 times for the Horita VLR-100 GV K2 Summit, LTC specification; Markertek, Evertz 5010; Horita VLR-100, manual. LTC cannot be read at standstill; the EVS glossary says so itself: "It can only be read if the tape is playing" EVS Multicam LSM, Operating Manual p. 182. This is the reason for the existence of VITC, readable "down to still frame and pause", and of its SDI successors ATC_VITC and ATC_LTC Horita VLR-100, manual; Cobalt Digital, About Timecodes v1.0.

A device that generates an LTC "following" a playback has only three possible behaviours in slow motion (inference from the research notes): (a) scale the bit rate with the speed — at 33% the fundamental falls to around 330–660 Hz, below 1/30 speed the readers lose lock, at standstill there are no transitions at all; (b) keep an LTC at nominal bit rate by repeating or skipping frame numbers; (c) stop or go silent. Behaviour (a) breaks exactly where EKO needs it most; (b) is decodable at any speed. Only one documented example of a VITC-to-LTC translator was found, and it does not follow the speed: "The LTC output of the VLR-100 in translation mode is always at play speed", and if the VITC is not at nominal speed the unit does not start incrementing its timecode Horita VLR-100, manual. Which of these behaviours an EVS server adopts is documented in no text read: this is the single most important point to measure on a real server.

4.3 LTC as PCM audio: reliable if it stays linear, unprocessed and co-delayed

Carrying LTC on an ordinary audio channel is a practice explicitly supported by manufacturers. Cobalt writes that this timecode "uses a spare analog or digital audio channel as its transport", that "The data is sufficiently slow enough to be reliably carried over balanced audio", and gives the example "LTC Encoder is selected as the source for Emb Ch 7" Cobalt Digital, About Timecodes v1.0. Its +LTC option receives an audio LTC on a balanced analogue, embedded or AES input and outputs it on an embedded or AES channel Cobalt Digital, +LTC supplement. Lawo states for V__matrix +tc_gen that "LTC can also be output as a digital audio signal", and the Evertz MIO-AES-LTC-D4 module can "de-embed up to 4x AES or a single LTC channel from an SDI source" TV Technology, Lawo V__matrix; Evertz HD9010TM. No EBU, SMPTE or EVS document, on the other hand, describes "LTC on an SDI embedded channel" as a production practice, and no manufacturer describes the complete chain "three EVS LTCs, stagebox, 2110-30, embedded channels 14/15/16" as a reference design: feasibility on each platform is an engineering inference, not a documented practice.

At 48 kHz, an LTC bit cell occupies 24 samples at 25 fps: the waveform is comfortably oversampled, and a 48 kHz linear PCM channel (AES3, SDI embedded, MADI, AES67 or Dante) passes it transparently as long as it stays linear and unprocessed (inference from the research notes). The Nevion Virtuoso carries embedded audio as "24-bit linear PCM at 48 kHz", and the NVISION 8500 documents 24-bit PCM transport with the C, U and V bits preserved Nevion Virtuoso SDI-IP-H25; GV NV8500, manual p. 69. The handling rules are well known: moderate level, no noise reduction, no equalisation, no compression, and separation from programme audio because of crosstalk; LTC "does usually not survive compressed codecs" Wikipedia, Linear timecode; Pomfort, Extracting LTC. Cobalt points out an input pitfall: "Card audio inputs will not center inputs with DC offset" Cobalt Digital, +LTC supplement.

The dangerous elements are those that a truck applies by default to programme audio: compressors and limiters, loudness processors, automatic gain, noise gates, high-pass filters, Dolby E or any lossy contribution codec, and any shuffling or mix-down that would sum the LTC channel with others; the LTC channels must be patched "raw", as a direct I/O patch and not through a console DSP path (inferences from the research notes). The example of Ultrimix-MXR is telling: each mixer input there carries a noise gate, a compressor-limiter and a four-band parametric equaliser Ross, UltriMix-MXR Direct Output Applications. On level, a square-wave LTC of 2 V peak to peak is about +2 dBu RMS, i.e. a peak at around −25 dBFS on a converter aligned at 0 dBFS = +24 dBu and at around −19 dBFS at +18 dBu (calculation of the notes from the levels quoted); a forum account places the useful minimum for readers at around −30 dB QLab, Google group.

The binding constraint for EKO is the relative delay between the LTC's audio path and the video it describes (inference from the research notes). The embedder itself costs about 1 ms: AJA 3G-AMA, 1,500 µs with SRC or 400 µs without according to Markertek, about 1,100 µs according to Full Compass — contradictory figures, all of the order of a millisecond Markertek, AJA 3G-AMA; Full Compass, AJA 3G-AMA. A field case shows the effect of a video delay: a recorder receiving the LTC directly and the video through a delayed SDI output of a camera recorded a timecode offset by about 3 frames DVXuser, 2014.

Dominant routers. In the English-speaking sample, Evertz (EQX baseband, EXE and NATX in IP, NEXX 12G) and Lawo V__matrix are the two most frequent cores, and Arista is the near-universal ST 2110 switch, Cisco appearing at Arena, F&F GTX-21 and Gravity Media. 9. Limits and points to test

Preservation of ATC on a re-embedded output. Only the GV Vega states in writing transparency to all ancillary data (HANC and VANC), without naming ATC; FOR-A documents the passing of all ANC with Quiet Switch disabled, and, on the gateway side, only Lawo names ATC; for Evertz EQX and NEXX, Sirius 800 AHP (module 5949), the NVISION 8500 embedder, the Imagine mux modules, Utah, Ross Ultrix and Riedel MediorNet, no document opened says what the embedder does with non-audio ancillary packets. The FOR-A case shows that a single menu setting restricts the ANC passed to a list in which ATC is not cited, so that its removal is probable but not written (inference from the research notes), and a modular SD-SDI embedder from Evertz, the 7720AE4, exposes HANC cleaning as a mode ("Clean and embed" removes all incoming HANC packets) FOR-A MFR-3100EX, specification sheet; Evertz 7720AE4, manual. ATC is moreover fragile under video processing: "if the SDI stream is up, down, or cross-converted, the timecode will be stripped from the output stream" Cobalt Digital, About Timecodes v1.0. Test to be carried out per router type: route the PGM to the embedder destination, replace channels 14 to 16, check on an analyser the presence and frame accuracy of ATC_LTC and ATC_VITC, with clean or quiet switch enabled then disabled. If an embedder turns out to rebuild the HANC, the three audio LTC channels become the fallback reference, since they are regenerated by the embedder itself (inference from the research notes).

Follow latency and behaviour during transitions. No manufacturer publishes a figure for the chain "vision mixer cut, control system, router follow". Switching in a synchronous router is executed at the next vertical switching point defined by SMPTE RP 168 (line 7 in 720p and 1080p, values taken from a partially illegible table, to be checked before quoting), and the only IP gateway figure found is Nevion's: "Break-before-make switching (1-2 frames additional latency)" SMPTE RP 168-2009; Nevion Virtuoso SDI-IP-H25. The reasoned, unmeasured budget is one to a few frames after the cut on SDI, with no guarantee of consistency from one cut to the next (inference from the research notes). Consequences to plan for in EKO, all of them inferences from the research notes: around each cut, the follow output shows the outgoing source for a few frames; the embedded ATC of the source actually received remains self-consistent, and its jump at the switch is the usable signature for detecting the cut on the input; during a mix or a wipe, a follow driven by the PGM bus crosspoint cuts only once, when the bus flips, whereas a follow driven by derived tally sees two sources and requires a priority rule; when the PGM is an internal source (still store, M/E re-entry, black), there is no router source to follow and a hold or fallback rule must be defined. To be tested on the target truck: cut, mix, wipe, fade to black, still store, M/E re-entry, key-only changes, and the moment at which the vision mixer flips the PGM bus crosspoint (start or end of an auto transition), not found in the accessible documentation.

Behaviour of the EVS LTC in slow motion, jog and still frame. This is the most important point to measure on a real server: bit rate scaled with speed, nominal bit rate with repeated numbers, or silence. The XT3 12.05 manual says only that the output "corresponds to PGM1"; the VIA generation is not confirmed EVS XT3, Hardware Technical Reference 12.05. A software decoder can track speed variations and reverse play — in libltc, "the speed is tracked dynamically" and a flag signals a frame read in the reverse direction — but nothing decodes at standstill libltc. Also to be recorded: the actual output level, the presence of a DC offset, and whether the LTC output already feeds other equipment: Pixotope advises against daisy-chaining LTC from one machine to the next and recommends a distribution amplifier Pixotope 24.2, Configure timecode.

Audio versus video offset. Published delays are rare: one or two lines for NVISION 8500 hybrid processing according to the manufacturer, 1 ms or less on the FOR-A MFR-8MADIPO and MFR-8AESPO cards, up to half a second of variable delay per mono channel with the Ross Ultrisync licence; no figure for Sirius AHP, the EQX TDM path, Platinum, Utah or MediorNet Miranda press release; FOR-A MFR-3100EX, specification sheet; SVG, 8 May 2020. In IP, transit adds a fixed delay (packet time, receiver buffer, any audio delay in the gateway: 0 to 250 ms at Sony) Sony NXLK-IP51Y. The frame sync options (Sirius S8AHP-VF, Evertz +FSE18, Utah SDIMOD-400S, NEXX-IO-R-FK-FSE) add up to one frame of video delay and must be disabled on the EKO destination; on MediorNet, where resynchronisation of the outputs is permanent, the offset must be measured (inferences from the research notes). Three rules follow, also inferred: the offset must be constant and calibrated once per truck; the three LTC channels must share exactly the same delay; no SRC, gain, mix, V-fade or "quiet switch" must touch these channels wherever it is avoidable. EKO's signed A/V OFFSET setting is the natural place for this calibration (inference from the research notes).

Availability of an analogue input. None is documented in-frame on Sirius 800, NVISION 8500, Ultrix, MicroN or .edge; it is documented at Evertz (block of 48 stereo pairs in a separate EMR frame), Utah (12 pairs), Imagine (16 pairs) and FOR-A (16 channels) (sources in section 5). The number of free line inputs on consoles and stageboxes, routing through the console (risk of processing, of mute, of snapshot recall, of re-patching between productions) and the delay of the console path remain to be established truck by truck; no source was collected on this point.

10. Questions to ask the truck engineer-in-charge (EIC)

  • What is the video router (brand, model, size) and which control system drives it: VSM, Cerebrum, Magnum, GV Orbit, TFC, hi, other?
  • Is there a free "embedder" output left (AHP card, hybrid, mux, Ultrimix, ST 2110 gateway output), and is the corresponding licence active on that output?
  • Can a custom 16-channel mono map be composed on it: international sound on the first channels, three discrete sources on 14, 15 and 16, without touching the transmission PGM?
  • Where do three line-level analogue signals come in: free line inputs on the console or a stagebox, router analogue card, free AES input or MADI port, Dante or AES67 network linked to the router? Can the path remain unprocessed (no dynamics, no filter, no SRC, no snapshot mute)?
  • Which EVS servers (XT3, XT4K, XT-VIA, XS-VIA, XT-GO): does each have an analogue LTC output, on which connector, and does it already feed other equipment?
  • What does this LTC output in slow motion at 33%, in jog, in reverse and in still frame? Does it follow PGM1 only?
  • Do the servers' SDI outputs carry ATC (HANC LTC, HANC VITC), and from which table (LTC or User)?
  • Does the embedder output keep the source's ATC? Can clean switch, quiet switch and frame sync be disabled on this destination?
  • What is the vision mixer (K-Frame, Kahuna, Sony XVS, Ross) and does the control system read its bus state (PGM bus crosspoints), or only tally?
  • Is a rule "the EKO destination follows the PGM bus crosspoint, on the video and ANC level only" achievable in the truck's control system? With what observed delay, and what behaviour during a mix, a wipe, and on an internal source (still store, M/E re-entry, black)?
  • Failing that, can a vision mixer Aux bus be linked to the PGM bus (Bus Linking, Internal Bus Link, Auto Follow), and does that Aux output pass the source's ancillary data?
  • What audio versus video offset is measured on the EKO destination, and is it stable from one production to the next?
